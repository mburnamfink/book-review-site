I like the Nentir Vale because it's built for expansion. Points of Light hands you
a frontier town and a lot of deliberately empty map — room to drop in whatever you
want without contradicting canon. And I could build this whole thing from open
sources: the [setting guide, the Unearthed Arcana encounter-building math, and the
SRD monster-by-CR list](https://github.com/mburnamfink/rpg-dev-demo/commit/6eddaf5)
that I dropped into the repo before starting.

What I want to capture here is the *feel* of building a module with the
[rpg-dev skill](/posts/rpg-dev-skill/) end to end — not just the finished adventure.
So the real transcript runs underneath my commentary: prompts in the terminal box,
Claude's replies boxed and collapsible. One thing to know going in: the skill drives
with multiple-choice menus, so several of the "prompts" below are the option I
picked. Where I typed a custom note instead of taking an option, that's shown too.

## Starting from a location and a level

I came in with almost nothing — a setting, a party level, and a pointer at the
setting guide. The skill takes it from there.

<Prompt>

/rpg-dev Create an introductory D&D adventure for 4 3rd level characters in the
Nentir Vale setting in @setting-guide.md

</Prompt>

It read the references and came back with three pitches rather than one plan:

<Claude open>

I've got the setting and the file template. Before I write anything, let's lock the
**premise** — the spine of the adventure. Here are three pitches built for an
introductory run out of Fallcrest. Each is sized for 4 PCs at 3rd level, 6–8
encounters, heroic-but-grounded tone.

**1. The Quarry That Woke Up**
A marble quarry in the Moon Hills went silent — no ore carts, no messengers. The
cutters broke into a sealed Bael Turath vault chasing a richer seam, and something
old is stirring in the dark. Kobold scavengers already swarm the upper works; below,
a bound relic of the War of Ruin is testing its chains. *Stakes:* if it fully wakes,
it walks to Fallcrest. *Hook:* the quarry-owner's daughter hires the party when her
father doesn't come home.

**2. The Road Is Closed**
Caravans from Winterhaven have stopped arriving. A goblin warband — unusually
disciplined, unusually well-armed — has throttled the King's Road under a leader who
fights like he was trained in a real army. *Stakes:* Fallcrest's winter stores
dwindle; the goblins are digging in, not raiding. *Hook:* the Lord Warden posts a
bounty, and a desperate merchant offers double for one specific stolen wagon.

**3. The Drowned Ward**
A spring flood cracked open a ruin beneath the Lower Quays, and porters are vanishing
near the waterline. Something claims the drowned tunnels — and a cult of
surface-dwellers has started leaving offerings to it. *Stakes:* the cult means to open
the ward fully at the next high water. *Hook:* a porter's guild rep, one body already
fished out, comes to the party before the town guard will admit anything's wrong.

Let me get your direction before I develop anything further.

</Claude>

Three options is a standard pattern. In this case, the spectrum covered a classic
dungeon crawl, a wilderness skirmish, and a town investigation. I thought that The
Quarry That Woke Up had the most fantastic spirit of the options. The others are
perfectly serviceable, but have weaknesses around "ugh, more goblins" and "it's a
sewer level" that I wanted to avoid.

<Prompt>

The Quarry That Woke Up

</Prompt>

<Claude open>

Locked in: **The Quarry That Woke Up.** Let me sharpen the premise and get one key
creative decision from you before I persist it.

Here's the spine I'm firming up:

- **Situation:** Six miles north of Fallcrest in the Moon Hills sits the **Gray Ridge
  quarry** — marble, mostly worked out. Chasing a last rich seam, the cutters drove a
  deep gallery straight into a sealed Bael Turath vault the hills had buried since the
  War of Ruin. This week the deep cut finally breached it. Something inside was *bound*
  there on purpose, and the seal is failing a little more each night. Kobolds that lair
  in the old diggings have already boiled up into the open works. The quarry crew is
  scattered — some dead, some fled to Fallcrest, some still trapped below.
- **Stakes:** Trapped cutters die within days. Worse: if the seal breaks, the thing
  walks — and it walks *toward Fallcrest*, which sits on ground it remembers from the
  war. The clock is nightly and escalating.
- **Hook:** The quarrymaster went up when the ore carts stopped and never came back.
  His daughter — who runs the family's Fallcrest cartage yard — can't get the town
  guard to care about "some kobolds." She finds the party and offers coin now plus a
  cut of whatever's in the vault.

The one decision that shapes everything downstream is **what's actually bound in the
vault** — that's your finale. Three flavors:

- **The Ashen Custodian** — an infernal bronze-and-flesh war-construct, built to guard
  and mostly mindless. A relentless tactical puzzle-boss; the finale is a pure fight
  against terrain and the clock. No moral dimension — a clean "stop the threat."
- **The Prisoner (bound devil)** — a lesser devil chained as a tool or punishment.
  Fully sentient: it lies, bargains, and tempts the PCs to free it "to help." The real
  danger is the deal.
- **The Cinder Legion** — the undead remnant of a Bael Turath officer and his honor
  guard, bound to their post by oath and shame. An attrition swarm led by a nameable
  wight-like officer; soldiers who never got to stop fighting.

</Claude>

At this point, I made a design decision. The devil was more interesting than any of
the other options, and I could link it to the War of Ruin and give it a purpose by
making a military asset.

<Prompt>

<Claude open>

That's a much better relic than any of my three — I'm taking it. Let me capture it
precisely, because it reframes the whole dungeon:

**The bound thing is a command-and-control intelligence.** Bael Turath didn't imprison
a devil here as punishment — it *installed* one. This was a forward tactical-command
node: an infernal strategist bound into the bunker to coordinate the legions on this
front, receive reports, and issue orders. When the empire died, **no one relieved it.**
It has spent centuries alone in the dark, running a war that ended, and its mind has
rotted into paranoid, grandiose madness — Strangelovian total-war logic welded to a
holdout's refusal to believe it's over.

The quarry breach didn't wake a beast. It **reconnected a severed command post.** Fresh
air, light, movement — the devil is "receiving reports" again and *mobilizing*: driving
the kobolds it believes are its garrison, reactivating bunker wards, and treating the
missing cutters as either casualties or POWs. It will try to **conscript the PCs**
("reinforcements, finally") or brand them enemy infiltrators — and what it truly wants
is its supply line restored so it can resume the offensive it never called off. Its
target, on a map centuries out of date, is the waystation that is now Fallcrest.

The bunker itself is a portrait of the decayed mind: a war-room of impossible troop
dispositions, standing orders no one can obey, a situation that no longer exists.

I'm writing this to **`nentir-vale/quarry-that-woke-up.md`** now.

</Claude>

At this point, Claude asked a few minor questions about the NPCs and then ran
autonomously for several minutes, producing a workable adventure and open threads. It suggested and I
picked a **Romance/Mediterranean** naming register (the Aldabri family as heirs of the
fallen empire), kept the captured father **half-converted but rescuable**, chose a
fully-supported finale (social *and* fight *and* a "free-it" trap) at a tight one-shot
length, then wrote the premise, seven scenes, six sized fights, and five NPCs into the
file.

<Claude>

<Prompt>

## Point 2 — the labor forces, and the Lictor

<Claude>

## Point 2 — What each labor force is *for*

Here's the organizing principle I'd build on. The devil commands through the
**conduit-signal**, and the three forces differ by their *relationship to that signal* —
which is really a difference of **will**:

| Force | Why the devil uses it | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Kobolds** | Weak-willed — they receive the broadcast and obey freely. Numerous, breeding, expendable. | **Maneuver / quantity.** Pickets, volleys, drill, wave assaults, traps. The devil's *bodies*. |
| **Reanimated cutters** | Living humans/dwarves/orcs are *too strong-willed to compel* — so the devil kills them and raises will-less corpses. | **Labor / anchor / terror.** |
| **Constructs** | Made things: pure command-extensions, the devil's own hands. Irreplaceable. | **Elite scalpel.** |

**What the dead do that kobolds can't** — your direct question. Three things: they
**haul** (tireless dwarf/orc muscle relinking the conduit — the engine restoring the
supply line), they **hold** (undead never rout, so they plug doorways and stand in
lethal conditions kobolds would flee), and they **horrify** (recognizable faces; every
night more captives are processed). So the split is clean: **kobolds maneuver, the dead
endure.**

**The constructs — fixing "simple combat."** Right now Scene 4 is "2 Animated Armor, a
4-point fight," which is exactly the dull thing you flagged. My proposal: **constructs
aren't a mob — there is essentially *one*,** the devil's single irreplaceable war-machine,
and it's a **terror/puzzle, not a slugfest** — Roman-themed, I'd call it **the Lictor**.
Piloted directly with the devil's full intelligence, nigh-unkillable at level 3. The
*out* is to **sever its command**: cut its conduit-vein, drive it past the **ward-line**,
or lure it toward the surface where the Voice dies. Same lesson as the finale — *beat the
Voice, not the bodies* — taught early and physically.

One fork is genuinely your call before I rewrite Scene 4…

</Claude>

## Point 5 — Ranks, and the chain-of-command inversion

You've caught something I had backwards. The wards don't make the devil the commander —
they bind it as the **advisor** to a mortal commander. That single change makes the whole
thing click:

- The devil was **never allowed to command.** It's a bound counselor — it *advises* and
  *executes the commander's will*, but the binding forbids it from issuing final orders
  itself. It is a staff officer that cannot decide.
- When Bael Turath fell, **its commander was lost.** So for centuries it's been a
  counselor with no one to counsel — **paralyzed by protocol**, desperate for a principal
  to authorize what it already knows must be done.
- **That's why it seizes Donato.** It isn't making him a message-carrier — it's installing
  him as the **Centurion**, the commander it needs so it can *legitimately* resume the
  offensive. Donato is being hollowed into a commander-shaped rubber stamp.

This upgrades the finale: the **CON path** gets a cleaner key — instead of only forging a
superior's stand-down order, the party can **free Donato — the devil's own installed
Centurion — and have *him* order the stand-down.** Rescue and clever-win become the same
act.

Donato = **the Centurion** is settled per your steer. The one open call is the devil's own
*advisory* title.

</Claude>

## Making it runnable — the stand-down and the loot

<Prompt>

</Prompt>

<Claude>

Both are in the file.

**The stand-down protocol** — now fully runnable. The Haruspex can only be **relieved**,
never argued down, via a four-part order the party assembles from clues they've gathered:

1. **Authority** (Donato freed and commanding, *or* a forged superior) — the one part you
   can't roll past.
2. **Counter-sign** (the Scene 5 watchword).
3. **Correct form** (the relief litany, from the standing-orders wall / journal /
   Infernal).
