The [rpg-dev skill](/posts/) turns Claude into a co-designer at the table. What
I want to capture here isn't the finished adventure — it's the *feel* of the
back-and-forth. So I've laid the actual transcript in underneath my commentary:
prompts in the terminal box, Claude's replies boxed and collapsible. Verbose
bits fold away.

## Starting from a vibe

I came in with almost nothing — a mood and a location. The thing I like about
the skill is that it doesn't demand a fully-formed pitch; it takes a fragment
and starts asking the right questions.

<Prompt>

I want a one-shot for 4 players set in a flooded cathedral. Gothic, a little
sad. Help me find the premise.

</Prompt>

Here's where it earned my trust. Instead of dumping a plot on me, it pushed back
with the questions I *should* have been asking myself:

<Claude open>

A flooded cathedral is a great stage — vertical, claustrophobic, and it puts a
clock on everything. Before I sketch a premise, three questions:

1. **Why are the players here?** Salvage, pilgrimage, or escape?
2. **What's the water doing?** Still rising, or already settled?
3. **Is anyone else down there** — survivors, scavengers, something worse?

<Thinking>

The location implies the mood but not the stakes. If I invent stakes now I'll
railroad the author. Better to surface the two or three decisions that fan out
into very different adventures, then let them pick.

</Thinking>

My instinct: **salvage against a rising tide**. It gives every scene a reason to
move and a reason to leave.

</Claude>

That "why are the players here" question is the one I always forget in my own
prep, and it reframed the whole thing. I picked salvage.

## Where it got verbose

Ask for NPCs and you get a *lot*. This is exactly the case the collapse is for —
I want the shape of the roster visible, but I don't want three paragraphs per
character wall-to-wall in the post.

<Prompt>

Give me three NPCs already down there when the players arrive. I want at least
one who shouldn't be trusted.

</Prompt>

<Claude>

Here are three, pitched to cross each other:

**Sister Wren** — the last cleric, refusing to leave until the reliquary is
recovered. Sincere, and her goal will get someone killed.

**Cato** — a rival salvager who got here first and has already mapped the safe
routes. Trades information for a cut. Not evil, just first in line for the exit.

**The Drowned Cantor** — was a chorister; is now something that sings the tide
higher. Do not trust the singing.

</Claude>

Three NPCs, one clearly the untrustworthy one, each with a lever on the others.
Folded up, it's a glance; expanded, it's the full pitch. That toggle is doing
the work I wanted the post to do.
