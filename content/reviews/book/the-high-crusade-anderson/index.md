*The High Crusade* is a swift little adventure that demonstrates Anderson's ability to tell a story in under two hundred pages that a contemporary author would pad out into a whole series.

An alien scout-craft arrives on Earth in 1354, planning to overawe the inhabitants as a prelude to conquering the world. Unfortunately for the would-be alien overlords from the Wersgorix Empire, the humans they first encounter are Sir Roger, Baron de Tourneville, and his men, prepared to embark for France as part of the Hundred Years' War. The aliens may have rayguns, but their personal security is sadly lacking, and the men-at-arms get inside the ship and cut everyone down, save one prisoner. Brother Parvus, the narrator of this chronicle, teaches the prisoner Latin and tries to fit this alien engineer into his mental framework, which tops out at demons, alchemy, and heavenly spheres.

While Parvus grapples with the theological implications, Sir Roger and his men develop a practical plan to use the immense craft to conquer France and then Jerusalem. Their prisoner betrays them and sets their course to the Wersgorix Empire, where they will meet the full might of the aliens and not just the few score crew on a scout ship. The English are outnumbered and facing enemies equipped with rayguns, atomic weapons, tanks, and every other contrivance of military science. All reason says they're on a one-way trip to slavery.

Reason fails. Sir Roger is a bold captain, and while weapons may have changed, treachery and strategy are much the same. The Wersgorix have become arrogant in their superiority, and by ambush, lies, and bold violence, England wins the day. One battle snowballs into an alliance with subjugated species, and the alliance builds into an avalanche which sweeps away Wersgorix dominion.

Two bits I particularly appreciated were using a trebuchet's relative invisibility to radar to create stealth artillery, and cracking a planetary command bunker by harrowing the rest of the planet and then sending in refugees to overwhelm its limited supplies. On the human side, Sir Roger has to contend with the fading affections of his wife Lady Catherine, who wishes to go home, and the knight Sir Owain, who lets romance and ambition lead him astray. For all their flash and thunder, the battles after Sir Roger takes the first planet are not particularly engaging. Either they're beyond Brother Parvus' comprehension, Poul Anderson's interests, or both. The marriage and betrayal subplots have to carry the emotional core of the book, and unfortunately feel tacked on.

Overall, this book is a deadpan comedy, with the punchline being that a bunch of 14th-century sword-wielding maniacs are just the people to bring down an interstellar empire. Anderson has better books—I'm particularly fond of his Viking epic [*The Broken Sword*](/book/the-broken-sword-anderson)—but *The High Crusade* is a quick and amusing snack of a tale.
