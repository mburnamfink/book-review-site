This novella stars Shiverick and a cast of strange and powerful sorcerers, gathering at one of their rare conclaves on a remote sandbar that appears only when the tides are right. Shiverick has never visibly cast a spell, and some of his colleagues have serious doubts that he is any kind of magician. He might just be a strange old man, except he's been attending these gatherings for hundreds of years,  Yet when one of the attendees is murdered, breaking a solemn truce, he must use the most powerful ability of all, deduction, to prevent Wizard War!

*"This Wizard War is fucked, man. I'm casting frostbite and level 2 poison, and I think I heard someone cast Power Word: Scrunch."*

This isn't the long-awaited next book in the Locke Lamora series, but I'm glad to read Lynch again. This is a charming little mystery. The ships that each sorcerer arrives in are an especially nice touch. While I didn't solve the mystery (I didn't particularly try), even with magic in the mix, a clever reader can see the shape of the murder and guess the killer. Logic beats mere mastery over the metaphysical fabric of existence.
